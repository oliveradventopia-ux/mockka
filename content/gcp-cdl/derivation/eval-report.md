Every answer was recorded at `high` confidence. One transcription slip was caught and corrected
**pre-key** (d2-08: the reasoning line described option B while the answer field carried D; the

**Reading this result honestly.** 83/83 is evidence of clean keying, and it is *not* evidence

the candidate is a business leader with none of it. What 83/83 does establish is that no item is
miskeyed in a way a domain-fluent reader would notice, and that no item has a co-correct option
strong enough to pull one. What it cannot establish — and what the missing cross-model check was
supposed to help with — is whether a *second* model family would have preferred a different
option anywhere. See §2.

**No dimension scores ≤2 anywhere in the bank**, so there are zero rubric bounces. The one bounce

**Dimension 3 and 4 at a clean 83/83 fives is a real finding, not a rubber stamp.** Concept
alignment: every item tests its declared `primary_concept` and the keyed reasoning *is* the
concept — the master inventory's one-concept-per-item discipline held through authoring.
Rationale traceability: the rationales argue rather than assert (every distractor rationale
concedes what is true about the option before saying where it fails, which is the house style
that makes them usable as study material), and the vocabulary is traceable — a mechanical check
confirmed **all 208 distinct `keywords` terms across the bank resolve to
`derivation/source-gcp-cdl-guide.md` or `derivation/master-inventory.md`**, zero orphans. For an
exam whose blueprint is three days old at authoring time, that is the check that matters.

**Where the 13 threes are, and what they mean.**

*Dimension 5 — nine items pitched right-vs-wrong rather than good-vs-best:* d1-01, d1-02, d1-06,
d1-08, d1-12, d1-14, d3-06, d3-11, d5-05. Seven of the nine are in domain 1. Two recurring
shapes:

1. **The stem states the definition and the item asks for the label.** d1-01 ("change how the
   organization operates and delivers value" → *digital transformation*), d1-02 (the stem
   enumerates multi-step goal pursuit; the key restates it), d5-05 (the security lead proposes
   "every request be verified whatever its origin"; the key is "zero-trust architecture, which
   verifies every request regardless of origin"), d1-08, d3-11.
2. **"Which statement is correct" recall items** — one true statement against three false ones,
   which is right-vs-wrong by construction: d1-12 (DNS), d1-14 (regions/zones).

*Dimension 2 — four items where a wrong option is disarmed by the stem's own wording rather than
by the concept:* d1-01 (three costumes of one error), d1-05 ("beyond more machines" pre-excludes
option B), d1-06 ("not about cost or capacity at all" pre-excludes two of three distractors,
leaving an effectively three-option item), d3-06 (see below).

**The open question for Gate 2 — is the domain-1 dim-5 cluster a defect or the correct pitch?**
The examiner's read, offered as input and not as a decision: **partly correct pitch, partly a
defect.** Domain 1 of this blueprint genuinely is definitional — it asks candidates to *identify*,
*describe* and *differentiate*, never to configure, and Google's own sample set is pitched at
that altitude. Recall items are legitimate there. But item d1-04 in the same domain manages
good-vs-best (four real cloud properties, one stated pain) and scores 4/4, so the altitude does
not *force* the flat pitch. The lever that would fix most of the cluster is narrow and does not
require reauthoring: **trim the definitional rider from the keyed option** where the stem already
supplies the definition (d1-02, d1-08, d5-05, d3-11), so the candidate must supply the mapping
rather than recognize it. That is the same fix aif-c01 took at its round-1 bounce
(keyed-option justification riders). It is *not* being demanded here — no threshold is breached
— it is put to Gate 2 as the cheapest available improvement.

**One construction finding worth naming: `gcp-cdl-d3-06`.** Options B and D both name
*uniqueness*, and B is phrased as a rebuttal ("Uniqueness would be broken **only if** the
duplicates were identical rather than spelled differently") rather than as an answer to the
question asked. The option set is therefore not parallel, and the B/D pairing telegraphs that
uniqueness is the contested axis. B carries a real misconception (that duplicates must be
identical), so it is not eliminable by everyone and the score is 3, not 2 — but a test-wise
reader eliminates it on form alone. Flagged to Gate 2 with a ratchet proposal below.

**Dimension 1 fours (6 items), for the record** — each is a distractor whose best case is close
rather than collapsed, and in every one the concept's own vocabulary settles it: d2-06 (drifting
copies genuinely are prevented by open formats — but they are the symptom silos produce, not the
named failure mode), d2-10 ("Standard until the pattern is understood, then revisit" is a real
practitioner's interim policy), d2-13 (on-demand refresh answers one of the stem's two
complaints), d3-01 ("the second is machine learning too" is *literally true* — generative AI is
built with ML — and loses only on insufficiency), d3-03 (operations is defensible if you classify
by which systems are touched), d6-01 (adding power, cooling and floor space to the purchase price
is a partially correct TCO). These are the bank's best items, not its weakest: a distractor with
a real case is what dimension 1 is measuring.

### 4 · Overlap screen — 1 bounce

Full report: `eval/overlap-report.md`. Mechanical screen of every item's stem + options +
rationales against all 1,599 lines of `derivation/*.md` at n = 5/6/7, plus a targeted pass against
the specific source fragments the derivation documents retain as quotations.

**Finding O-1 → `gcp-cdl-d4-10` bounces (bounce 1 of 2), `defect_class: source-phrase-reuse`.**
The item's `rationale.correct` opens "Cloud Run is for teams that want their **own language and
tools** with **minimal infrastructure management**". `derivation/source-gcp-cdl-samples.md:151`
records, in quotation marks, that Google's own published sample item 4.2 turns on the phrase
"*my own language and tools, minimal infrastructure management*". The Artefact B vocabulary
exception was tested and does not apply — neither phrase appears in the blueprint distillation,
and the master inventory re-expresses the same idea in its own words as "own-tools containers"
(C-055), which shows the borrowing was never necessary. This is vendor item prose, and the repo
hard rule is unconditional: never reproduce source text. The required fix is confined to that one
clause; the stem, options and key are independently expressed and stay as they are. Dimensions
1–6 are unaffected (5/4/5/5/4/5 at bounce).

**Root cause is S1, not S3.** The authoring session behaved correctly — it worked from the
derivation artefact, as the clean-room rule requires — and the artefact is what carried the
vendor's words forward, despite that document's own header asserting "No question text, option
text or rationale prose is reproduced here". Two such quotations survive in it (lines 151 and
157); the second, "without making any changes to the code", did **not** leak — `gcp-cdl-d4-02`
re-expresses that constraint independently. Scrubbing the artefact is distillation work, outside
the examiner's write surface, and is raised for Gate 2.

**Cleared explicitly:** the 41 seven-gram hits against derivation text are all either canonical
blueprint vocabulary (the Artefact B exception — IaaS/PaaS/SaaS, the three threat-intelligence
sources, the CapEx/OpEx/TCO triad, and so on) or Mockka's own prose reused between Mockka's own
documents. Concept-level correspondence between the d4 items and vendor sample items 4.1–4.5 is
**not** a finding: both derive from the same public objective bullets via the master inventory,
and the scenarios, verticals, options and keys are original. The screen judges expressed text,
not shared syllabus.

**Ratchet proposals** (`00-pipeline.md#ratchet`) — `source-phrase-reuse` has now appeared in two
exams (ccao-f round 1: 2 items; gcp-cdl round 1: 1 item), which is a class climbing the ladder:

- **R-1 (methodology).** `01-source-distillation.md` should forbid quoted source-prose fragments
  in Artefact-A classification documents outright — name the concept and the pattern, never quote
  the item.
- **R-2 (validator).** A `derivation-quote-leak` check: extract every quoted run of ≥5 words from
  `derivation/*.md`, shingle it, and fail any bank item reproducing a ≥4-gram of it unless the run
  also appears in the blueprint distillation (Artefact B exception). This finding and both ccao-f
  findings would have been caught at S4.
- **R-3 (authoring guide, from the d3-06 finding).** An option-parallelism rule: no two options in
  a single_choice item may name the same concept as their head term, and no option may be phrased
  as a rebuttal of another option rather than as an answer to the stem. Cheaply checkable later
  if the pattern recurs.

aligned to its concepts and fully traceable in vocabulary; its one systematic soft spot is
difficulty pitch in the definitional domain, and its one hard finding is a phrase inherited from
an S1 artefact that should never have retained it.
